# Decision log

Choices made while building, newest first. Each entry says what changed from the original plan and why.

| Date | Decision | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 15 Nov (display) | Patch pictures and low-Sun previews are drawn at 30 m (display_resolution_m) from the original maps, in a window with exactly the footprint of each 60 m patch (checked: the 30 m window averaged 2 x 2 equals the 60 m patch). Every score, fingerprint and lighting similarity still uses the 60 m grid. | The 60 m patches are only 64 x 64 cells and looked blurred when enlarged; Copernicus is 30 m natively and the LOLA and CTX maps are finer, so the pictures can show real detail without changing any result. |
| 15 Nov (quality) | Sun hours are vectorised at 1-minute steps (no 5-minute quantisation). Calendar and month rule use NASA POWER T2M_MIN_AVG / T2M_MAX_AVG (mean daily low and high); the earlier T2M_MIN / T2M_MAX are the coldest and warmest hour of the month, now labelled as such where still shown. Months are suggested only when at least one month passes BOTH the rain and temperature rules and the best such month has at least 10 more low-sun minutes a day than the median such month; there is no fallback, and the dossier states the reason (74 of 450 sites get a suggestion). Each Moon target exports the JETT5 area's monthly low-sun minutes for its band. Where 90 - abs(lat) - 1.54 < 0 the app says the Sun never rises more than the band top and for part of the year stays below the horizon. The SPA check compares our true elevation with the report's unrefracted topocentric e0 (39.8720 deg). The masked crater test keeps 3 query craters, too few to interpret, and says so. | Review of the lighting and calendar outputs: earlier months were picked on 5-minute steps, monthly extremes and a fallback to all months, which overstated the advice. |
| 14 Nov (phase 5) | Shortlist downloads (CSV, GeoJSON, KML) follow the active filter and priority order. A printable field-test brief per site. A "Before you go" box on every dossier: a generic line, plus region notes only where an official source was read: Antarctic Treaty Secretariat (McMurdo), NPS Death Valley research permits, and the Office of Hawaiian Affairs on Maunakea. | Planners need the list in their own tools. Notes without a source could be wrong, so the other regions show the generic line only. |
| 14 Nov (phase 4) | Suggested months are our rule in config/rehearsal.yaml: months with at most 1.0 mm/day of NASA POWER rain and a mean daily low of at least -25 °C and high of at most 35 °C; the 3 with the most low-sun hours (Moon targets) or daylight hours (Mars) win. If no month is that dry, the temperature band alone applies; if none fits it either, all months are ranked. Sun hours use the 15th of each month of 2025 in 5-minute steps. | A transparent, adjustable rule; labelled in the app as our rule, not safety advice. Without the temperature band the rule picked 43-47 °C summer months at Harrat Khaybar and Death Valley and -45 °C months at McMurdo. |
| 14 Nov (phase 3) | Low-sun lighting analog. Noon Sun band = (90 - abs(lat)) +/- 1.54 deg, clipped at 0. Shadow fingerprint at 1, 2, 3, 5 and 10 deg (our choice) over 36 azimuths, rays marched over a 64 px (3.84 km) margin with nearest-pixel sampling, a curvature drop of d^2/2R (Moon 1,737.4 km, Earth 6,371 km), and attached shadow where the slope toward the Sun exceeds tan(e). Lighting similarity = 100 x (1 - mean abs difference) over the elevations inside the band. It is a separate panel and is never added to the similarity score. Mars: not applicable. Each Earth window is blanked wherever its target patch's window is, so both see the same surroundings: the LOLA site maps are small, and 18-100% of target patches (by target) had a cut-off margin against 14-26% of Earth patches, which biased the comparison. At 1-2 deg most rough terrain is largely in shadow on both bodies, so scores bunch near the top; the app shows one decimal and the shadow shares. Earth Sun position from NOAA's equations, checked against the NREL SPA example. The preview uses the band's middle elevation at azimuth 315 with one shared stretch. | JETT5 ran night EVAs to test a variety of lighting conditions (NASA SVS interview). The south-pole Sun stays low, so shadow behaviour is a second, independent analog question. The brief's spotlight detail is not stated in the NASA or USGS sources, so the app does not repeat it. The refraction note quotes NOAA (0.833 deg assumed for sunrise and sunset) rather than an unsourced "half a degree". |
| 14 Nov (phase 2) | "How the usual sites compare": each well-studied region's best patch per target on the app's own score and sigma, ranked among searchable patches and among all valid Earth grid patches (masks off: no land-cover or relief filter). The SP Crater region is marked as lying in the San Francisco Volcanic Field, where NASA's JETT5 Artemis III field test ran (13-17 May 2024; USGS, NASA). The top 3 shortlisted sites scoring above its best patch are listed with the groups where each is closer. | Shows how established sites fare without changing any score. The wording is "closer terrain match on our fingerprint", never "better than NASA's choice". JETT5 sources name the volcanic field, not SP Crater itself. |
| 14 Nov (phase 1) | Added PGDA LOLA 5 m targets Haworth, Site06 "Nobile rim 1" and DM2 "Nobile rim 2". artemis_iii_region is set only for exact name matches with NASA's 28 Oct 2024 list (Haworth, Malapert Massif, Nobile Rim 1, Nobile Rim 2): 4 of 9 regions. Site11 "de Gerlache rim" stays unlabelled because NASA lists "de Gerlache Rim 2". | The other five regions have no LOLA 5 m site map in the PGDA folder. The Earth index, scaler and PCA are fit on Earth patches only, so the new targets do not change existing scores (checked by scripts/regression_guard.py). |
| 14 Nov (phase 0) | Climate-aware vegetation mask. Trees, crops, built-up, wetland and mangrove always count toward the 20% limit. Shrub, grass and moss/lichen count only where NASA POWER annual precipitation (PRECTOTCORR, nearest cell of the regional climatology grid, one request per region widened to the endpoint's 2° minimum) exceeds 500 mm/yr. | WorldCover labels sparse desert scrub and spinifex as shrub or grass, so counting them everywhere removed arid analog land: the Pilbara had 0 searchable patches, Meteor Crater 545 of 2,390 and SP Crater 520 of 2,345. 500 mm/yr is the upper bound of semiarid land in the USGS desert primer (Deserts: Geology and Resources, https://pubs.usgs.gov/gip/deserts/what/: "semiarid lands have a mean annual precipitation of between 250 and 500 millimeters"; the page blocks automated fetching, so the sentence was checked against the search-engine copy). After the change 1 of the 90 top-10 sites is more than 20% vegetated within 2 km: Haworth #10, Askja (23%, all shrub, grass or moss). |
| 14 Nov (review) | The land-cover mask counts every vegetated WorldCover class (trees, shrubs, grass, crops, built-up, wetland, mangrove, moss and lichen) and also drops patches with more than 50% snow and ice. The rover-mobility and steep-slope test lines now need at least 50% bare ground within 2 km. | The old mask counted only trees, crops and buildings, so grass and shrubland passed: 22 of the 60 top-10 sites were more than 20% vegetated, and two top-10 sites were on glacier ice. The dossier's land-cover line already used the full class list. |
| 14 Nov (review) | Sites in Antarctica, where WorldCover has no tiles, get a breaks-down line: "No land-cover data south of 60°S: this patch may be glacier ice; check the imagery." | McMurdo sites had no land-cover data and their dossiers did not say so. |
| 14 Nov (review) | Novelty labels are now "Near a known analog" (within 50 km of a catalogue entry, or in a well-studied analog region) and "Outside our analog catalogue". Three catalogue entries were added, each with a quoted Wikipedia sentence: Makhtesh Ramon (D-MARS), Salar de Pajonales and Licancabur. Dhofar, Dallol and Pilbara were skipped because no sentence stating analog use was found. | One point cannot stand for an analog area as large as the Atacama: Malapert's top match near Yungay was labelled "less studied" at 60 km from the Yungay point. "Less studied" also claimed more than the data shows, since it only means outside our catalogue. |
| 14 Nov (review) | V1 adds a combined row at the Moon targets' median σ and an AUC against hard negatives (non-crater patches from the other crater tiles). V3 adds terrain/embedding splits of 0.4/0.6 and 0.8/0.2. The app keeps 0.6/0.4. | The old 0.976 headline was in effect the embedding alone, because the Earth-to-Earth σ drives the terrain term to near zero. The 0.6/0.4 split was set before the benchmark existed; changing it after seeing the results would tune the method on its own test set. |
| 14 Nov (review) | Every patch hillshade uses one shared contrast stretch: the 1st–99th percentile over all exported patches. Target overview images keep a per-image stretch. This replaces the per-image stretch below. | Stretching each image separately made a smooth Earth patch look as rough as a steep lunar patch in the side-by-side view. A shared range keeps brightness comparable; some steep lunar patches now render brighter. |
| 14 Nov | Added the de Gerlache rim (Site11) and Leibnitz beta plateau (Site20) targets after the core app was complete. | Both use the same PGDA LOLA product, so the pipeline needed no changes. |
| 14 Nov | Client-side "priorities" sliders re-rank only the exported top-50 shortlist by weighted explanation bars. | Planners weigh terrain factors differently. The similarity search itself is unchanged, and the UI says so. |
| 14 Nov | V1 crater benchmark: the queries are patches centred on each crater. The candidates are regular grid patches only. A hit is a grid patch whose 3.84 km window fully contains another crater's rim (\|dx\|, \|dy\| ≤ 1.92 km − D/2). | The first version also used the crater-centred patches as answers. The embedding then ranked another crater first for every query, which is an artefact of exact centring. The rule was fixed from geometry before rerunning. Both versions are disclosed on the Validation page. |
| 14 Nov | V1's combined row is labelled "Earth-to-Earth σ". | With σ = 0.163 the terrain term is near zero for crater pairs, so that row tracks the embedding. It is not the exact ranker used for Moon targets. |
| 14 Nov | Novelty is "well studied" within 50 km of a catalogued analog. The catalogue has 21 points: 14 whose Wikipedia article states an analog or astronaut-training use (sentence quoted), plus the 7 remaining well-studied regions from the project list. | This is a transparent, sourced rule. Qaidam Basin is in the catalogue because its article says the China Geological Survey studies it as a Mars analogue, even though the original plan listed it as less studied. |
| 14 Nov | σ in s_morph = exp(−d²/2σ²) is the median distance from the *query's* patches to their nearest candidate. For Earth-to-Earth queries, neighbours in the same region are excluded. | Using the plan's Earth-only nearest-neighbour σ (0.163), s_morph was 0.001–0.05 for every Moon match. The steep lunar massifs sit far from all Earth terrain, so the ranking would have been embedding-only. Fixed before any benchmark run. |
| 14 Nov | The explanation bars are percentiles against 200,000 random cross-region Earth patch pairs. | A Gaussian kernel per group collapses in low dimension. A percentile reads directly: 90 = closer than 90% of random Earth pairs. |
| 14 Nov | Equal group weights are applied as the mean squared difference within each group. | The slope group has 14 features and relief has 2. Plain Euclidean distance would let slope dominate. |
| 14 Nov | Hillshade images are contrast-stretched (1st–99th percentile) for display only. | A uniform 19° lunar slope facing the light rendered almost white. Features are unaffected. |
| 14 Nov | Each Earth region is a 100 km × 100 km box centred on its cited coordinate (60 km for crater tiles), mosaicked from all intersecting Copernicus tiles. Patches in overlapping boxes go to the nearer centre. | 1° tiles cut through features and shrink with latitude. Equal-area boxes keep regions comparable. |
| 14 Nov | WorldCover is read at about 20 m, then mode-resampled to 60 m. | Reading 10 m windows at Svalbard latitudes needs more than 600 MB per region. 20 m is still three times finer than the analysis grid. |
| 14 Nov | McMurdo Dry Valleys runs without land-cover masks. | ESA WorldCover has no Antarctic tiles. The region is flagged in the data and in the app. |
| 14 Nov | Benchmark craters: every PASSC structure 0.8–3.5 km across, exposed, and younger than 10 Ma. Seven pass. The crater centres use Wikipedia coordinates, which agree with PASSC's arc-minute values. | This is an objective rule set before any results. passc.net is offline (parked domain), so the tables were read from Internet Archive snapshots. |
| 14 Nov | The Yungay (Atacama) centre comes from the PANGAEA dataset of the Yungay hyperarid soil site. | No Wikipedia article gives coordinates for the Yungay research area. |
| 14 Nov | Earth imagery in the app uses NASA GIBS Landsat WELD (2000 annual composite, about 38 m). | It is the sharpest true-colour layer GIBS serves globally in Web Mercator. |
| 14 Nov | Static site on GitHub Pages; FastAPI only as a stretch goal. | No server can fail during judging. |
| 14 Nov | Common 60 m grid and 3.84 km patches. | LOLA 5 m pixels are mostly interpolated, and one grid makes slopes comparable across bodies. |
